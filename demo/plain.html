Moving the homelab to a single NUC

2026-11-02 · 6 min read · tagged homelab, self-hosting

For three years the "lab" was four machines, two of which existed mostly to keep the other two company. This weekend I collapsed everything onto one Intel NUC with 64 GB of RAM and a pair of NVMe drives. Here is what moved, what broke, and what I'd do again.

What runs where

Everything is a container now, managed by one compose.yaml per service and a Makefile that ties them together.

ServiceBeforeAfterNotes
Caddypi-edgenucwildcard cert via DNS-01
Postgresold-towernuc17, WAL to restic
Home Assistantpi-hanucZigbee stick passed through
Jellyfinold-towernucQuickSync transcode

The migration

  1. Snapshot every volume with restic backup.
  2. Bring services up on the NUC one at a time, pointed at a copy of the data.
  3. Flip DNS. Wait. Flip it back twice.
  4. Power off the old machines and enjoy the silence.

The only real surprise was Postgres. A straight pg_dump of 40 GB took long enough that I switched to streaming it:

ssh old-tower 'pg_dump -Fc notes' | pg_restore -d notes --no-owner -j 4

Lesson: test the restore before you need it. The backup you have never restored is a rumour.

Caddy config

The whole edge is now ten lines:

*.lab.example.net {
	tls {
		dns cloudflare {env.CF_API_TOKEN}
	}
	@ha host ha.lab.example.net
	reverse_proxy @ha 127.0.0.1:8123
	@jf host tv.lab.example.net
	reverse_proxy @jf 127.0.0.1:8096
}

Power draw

  • Before: about 210 W at idle across four boxes.
  • After: 18 W idle, 45 W while transcoding.
  • Saving: roughly 1,600 kWh a year, which pays for the NUC in under two.
Hardware list
  • Intel NUC 13 Pro, i7-1360P
  • 2 × 32 GB DDR4-3200
  • 2 × 2 TB NVMe, mirrored with ZFS
